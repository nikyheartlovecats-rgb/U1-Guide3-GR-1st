Theoretical Explanation: Family, Have, and Who's

Welcome to Unit 3! Today we are learning how to talk about our family members, count them, and ask who people are using English.

Part A: The Verb "To Have" (Grammar I)

We use have and don't have (do not) to talk about our family members and siblings.

  • I have one sister. / I have two brothers.
  • I don't have any sisters. / I don't have a big sister.
  • Question: How many sisters do you have?

Part B: Family Members Vocabulary

Key words to describe our relatives: mother, father, sister, brother, baby sister, baby brother, grandmother, grandfather, family, big, small.

Part C: "He / She" and Questions with "Who's" (Grammar 2)

We use special pronouns and question structures to identify family members in photographs or real life:

  • Who's = Who is (Who's he? / Who's she?)
  • He's my brother Jack. (He is = He's)
  • She's my sister Ana. (She is = She's)

Resolved Examples

Review these models based on Our World 1 to understand how to apply the grammar rules.

Basic Level Examples

Example 1: Affirmative "To Have"

Input: Complete: "I have two _______." (brothers)

Solution: I have two brothers. (Refers to male siblings).

Example 2: Negative "To Have"

Input: Fill in the blank: "I _______ have any brothers." (don't / do)

Solution: I don't have any brothers. ("don't" means do not).

Example 3: Baby family member

Input: Complete: "One. I have a baby _______." (sister)

Solution: One. I have a baby sister.

Example 4: Identifying with She's

Input: Look at photo 1: "She's my _______." (sister)

Solution: She's my sister.

Example 5: Identifying with He's

Input: Look at photo: "He's my _______." (grandfather)

Solution: He's my grandfather.
Intermediate Level Examples

Example 6: Describing Lisa's family

Input: Complete: "Hi, I'm Lisa. I have a big _______." (family)

Solution: Hi, I'm Lisa. I have a big family.

Example 7: Complex sentence with big/little

Input: Complete: "I have a little _______." (brother)

Solution: I have a little brother.

Example 8: Combining brother and sister

Input: Complete: "And I have a _______ sister, too!" (baby)

Solution: And I have a baby sister, too!

Example 9: Who's question contraction

Input: What does "Who's" mean?

Solution: "Who's" means "Who is".

Example 10: Describing grandmother and brother

Input: Complete: "She's my grandmother, and he's my _______." (brother)

Solution: She's my grandmother, and he's my brother.
Advanced Level Examples

Example 11: Miguel's family analysis

Input: Based on Miguel's family web (Mother: Juana, Father: Angel, 2 brothers: Pablo, Luis, 1 sister: Rosa, 1 baby sister: Lola): Answer: "Is Miguel's family big or small?"

Solution: It is big! (Because he has many family members).

Example 12: Counting brothers from web

Input: "How many brothers does Miguel have?"

Solution: He has 2 brothers (Pablo and Luis).

Example 13: Counting sisters from web

Input: "How many sisters does Miguel have?"

Solution: He has 2 sisters (Rosa and Lola the baby sister).

Example 14: Personalizing "To have" response

Input: Complete for yourself: "How many sisters do you have? I _______."

Solution: Example: "I have one sister." or "I don't have any sisters."

Example 15: Identifying multiple relations

Input: "She's my mother, and he's my father. Together they are my _______."

Solution: Together they are my parents / or part of my family.

Critical Reflection Questions

Think about these questions with your teacher or classmates to deepen your understanding!

Question 1

Why is it important to know how to describe our family members in English?

Guidance: It helps us share who we love, talk about people who live with us, and introduce our family to new friends!

Question 2

What is the difference between saying "I have a sister" and "I don't have any sisters"?

Guidance: "I have" means you possess that family member, while "I don't have" means zero sisters.

Question 3

When do we use "He's" instead of "She's" when talking about family?

Guidance: We use "He's" for males (like brothers, fathers, grandfathers) and "She's" for females (like sisters, mothers, grandmothers).
